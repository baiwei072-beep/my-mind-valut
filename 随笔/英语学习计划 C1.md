# English Speaking Plan: B1-B2 → C1

**Current level:** B1–B2  
**Target:** C1  
**Timeline:** 8–12 months of consistent practice

---

## Existing Habit (Keep & Level Up)

**Morning read-aloud — 10–15 min daily**

Don't just read, *perform* it. Vary pace, stress key words, pause for effect.

Best sources: The Economist, BBC News, TED Talk transcripts — not novels. C1 lives in argument and opinion, not narrative.

---

## 3 Practice Blocks

### Block A — Shadowing | 10 min, 4x/week

Shadow a native speaker line by line: play 5 seconds → pause → repeat exactly, matching rhythm, linking, and reduction ("gonna", "wanna", "kinda").

**Sources:** TED Talks, Huberman Lab podcast clips, NPR

### Block B — Structured Monologue | 10 min, 3x/week

Pick a prompt, talk for 2–3 minutes without stopping. Record yourself. Listen back once. Don't script it — the discomfort is the point.

**Prompt ideas:**
- "Explain a decision you regret"
- "Argue for/against remote work"
- "Describe a complex problem you solved"

### Block C — Weekly Input Binge | 30–45 min, 1x/week

Watch one full interview or documentary without subtitles. Note 5 phrases you'd never say naturally. Use them in your next monologue.

---

## Milestones

| Month | What changes                                                             |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1–2   | Smoother linking, less pause-filling with "um"                           |
| 3–4   | You start reaching for precise words instead of paraphrasing             |
| 5–6   | You can argue a point with hedging language ("It could be argued that…") |
| 8–10  | Register control — you adjust formality naturally                        |
| 12    | C1 territory: sustained complex speech, minimal L1 interference          |

---

## Key Warning

Treating read-aloud as the main workout will stall progress. It builds pronunciation and rhythm, but C1 requires *spontaneous* production under pressure. **Block B (monologue) is non-negotiable.**
